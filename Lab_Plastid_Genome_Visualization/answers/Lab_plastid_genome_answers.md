## Part D. Examination of the Plastid Genome Map

The *Camellia sinensis* chloroplast genome has a circular and quadripartite structure consisting of a large single-copy (LSC) region, a small single-copy (SSC) region, and two inverted repeat regions (IRa and IRb). The map shows protein-coding genes, tRNA genes, and rRNA genes distributed around the genome. Gene arrows indicate the direction of transcription.

The LSC is the largest region and contains many protein-coding genes involved in photosynthesis, transcription, and other plastid functions. The SSC contains several genes, including genes of the NADH dehydrogenase complex. The IR regions contain duplicated genes, including rRNA and ribosomal protein genes. The GC content graph shows variation in GC content around the genome.

## Part E. Questions and Answers

### 1. What is the scientific name of your chosen plant and its plastid genome accession number?

The scientific name is *Camellia sinensis*, and its plastid genome accession number is **NC_020019.1**.

### 2. What is the total length of the plastid genome in base pairs?

### 3. Can you identify the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions on your map? Briefly describe their positions.

Yes. The map shows the typical four-part organization of a plastid genome:

- **LSC:** 86,645 bp; the largest single-copy region.
- **SSC:** 18,276 bp; the smaller single-copy region.
- **IRa:** 26,091 bp; one of the two inverted repeat regions.
- **IRb:** 26,091 bp; the second inverted repeat region.

The two IR regions separate the LSC and SSC regions and are located opposite each other in the circular genome.

### 4. Give three examples of genes located in the LSC region.

Three examples of genes located in the LSC region are **rbcL, psbA, and rpoC2**.

- **rbcL** encodes the large subunit of Rubisco.
- **psbA** encodes a reaction-center protein of photosystem II.
- **rpoC2** encodes a subunit of the plastid-encoded RNA polymerase.

### 5. Give at least one example of a gene located in the SSC region.

One example is **ndhF**, which is located in the SSC region. It encodes a subunit of the NADH dehydrogenase-like complex involved in photosynthetic electron transport.

### 6. Give at least one example of a gene that occurs within an inverted repeat region. Is the gene shown more than once because of the duplicated IR regions?

One example is **rpl2**, which occurs within an inverted repeat region. Yes. Because IRa and IRb are duplicated regions of the chloroplast genome, genes located within the IR regions can occur in two copies, with one copy in each IR.

### 7. Choose one photosynthesis-related gene visible on your map. State its gene name and its general biological function.

**rbcL** is a photosynthesis-related gene visible on the map. It encodes the large subunit of Rubisco, an enzyme involved in carbon fixation during photosynthesis.

### 8. What does the direction of the gene arrows or gene orientation tell you about transcription on the plastid genome?

The direction of the arrows indicates the orientation of transcription of each gene. Genes with arrows pointing in one direction are transcribed in that direction, while genes pointing in the opposite direction are transcribed from the opposite DNA strand. This shows that genes in the plastid genome are not all transcribed in the same direction.

### 9. Look at the GC content graph. Does GC content appear uniform across the entire plastid genome, or does it vary among regions? Describe what you observe without over-interpreting it.

The GC content does not appear completely uniform across the genome. The GC content graph shows fluctuations around the circular genome, with some regions appearing relatively higher or lower than others. The variation appears gradual across different portions of the genome.

### 10. Why is a graphical plastid genome map useful compared with viewing only the raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations?

A graphical plastid genome map makes the organization of the genome easier to understand. It allows the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions to be seen clearly and shows the locations and orientations of genes around the circular genome. It also makes duplicated genes, different gene categories, and the GC-content pattern easier to recognize than a raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations.
